Deal with Bullies

"If people are trying to bring you down, it only means that you are above them."

- Katie Styzek and Glenn Carreau

Why is it important to avoid Bullying people?

Bullying can have harmful and long lasting consequences for you. Besides the physical effects of bullying, you may experience emotional and mental health problems, including depression and anxiety, that can lead to substance abuse and decreased performance in College.

Following these steps can help you to deal with bullies

  • Deflect the bully with humor.
    Laugh it off to show the bully they can't control your emotions. When a bully starts bothering you, take a deep breath and start laughing. Laugh at everything they say—the worse the insult, the harder you should chuckle. After laughing, simply turn around and walk away without adding anything else. This tactic frustrates bullies because they want you to cry, not laugh.
  • Avoid the bully as much as possible.
    When your bully doesn't see you, they won't bully you. Try to avoid them in college and social situations. As the saying goes: out of sight, out of mind!
  • Ignore the bully and walk away.
    Pretend you can't hear and deny them the satisfaction of getting to you. Do your best not to show any reaction in front of the bully—just walk away if you can.
  • Stand up for yourself.
    Gather your courage and firmly tell the bully, "no." When a bully approaches you, stand tall and be brave—or pretend, even if you don't feel brave. Loudly and confidently say, "No! Stop it," to them, and then walk away.
  • Stick close to your friends.
    Bullies are less likely to pick on you when you're not alone. Hang out with your friends as much as possible and travel in pairs (or groups) each day. The more time you spend with others, the less your bully will target you.
  • Report the bullying to an authority figure.
    Tell an adult that you trust so they can help you deal with your bully. It can be nerve-wracking to tell someone else about your situation, but speaking to a trusted adult will make you feel less afraid.
  • Practice self-affirmations and Stay Positive.
    Recite affirmations and remind yourself to be proud of who you are. Build up your self-confidence to withstand a bully's mean remarks. Practice daily self-affirmations, reminding yourself of all your strengths, talents, and positive qualities. Eliminate negative self-talk too, and say only kind things to yourself. Resist the urge to insult your bully and sink down to their level. While you can definitely call someone out for bullying and find holes in their argument, never resort to behavior like theirs. spend time each day reflecting on the things you love and are grateful for to get yourself in a positive headspace no matter what the bully says.
